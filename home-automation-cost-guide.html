How Much Does Home Automation Really Cost? (2026 Guide)

Sedra Electric · 10 August 2026 · 6 min read

"How much does a smart home cost?" is the first question almost every client asks — and the honest answer is: it depends. Unlike buying a single product, home automation is a system tailored to your space, your priorities and your budget. This guide breaks down what actually drives the cost so you can plan with confidence.

What drives the price

  • Size & number of zones: more rooms and lighting circuits mean more devices and wiring.
  • Which systems you include: lighting only is far cheaper than a full package (lighting + climate + security + audio + shades).
  • Technology (KNX vs wireless): KNX costs more upfront but delivers long-term reliability and expandability.
  • Hardware brand & quality: premium keypads, touch panels and speakers raise the price.
  • Project stage: installing during construction/fit-out is more efficient than retrofitting a finished home.
The single biggest cost lever is scope. Deciding what you truly need — and phasing the rest — is how smart clients control budget without compromising quality.

The three typical tiers

Entry / Essentials — Smart lighting and basic security for an apartment or a few key rooms. Wireless is often the sweet spot here: fast to install, no major works.
Mid-range / Integrated — Lighting, climate, shades and security working together with scenes, across a whole home. This is where automation starts to feel seamless.
Luxury / Turnkey — Full KNX villa: every system integrated, home cinema, multi-room audio, energy management, solar and EV. Engineered end-to-end during construction.

KNX vs wireless — the cost trade-off

Wireless is cheaper and faster to install, ideal for finished apartments. KNX is a wired, globally certified standard — higher initial cost, but rock-solid, expandable and built to last for years. For a villa under construction, KNX almost always wins on lifetime value.

Don't forget ongoing value

A good system isn't just install cost. Factor in warranty, local support and spare parts — and the savings a smart home returns through efficient lighting, climate control and (with solar) lower energy bills.

Rather than chasing a fixed price online, request a site assessment and a detailed, itemised quote for your home. That's the only way to know the real number — with no surprises.

How to budget wisely

  • Rank your priorities (security, energy saving, comfort).
  • Design for the full home but install in phases if needed.
  • Choose an expandable, certified system so you're never locked in.
  • Confirm warranty and local support before you sign.
