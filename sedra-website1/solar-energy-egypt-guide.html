Solar Energy for Homes in Egypt: Savings, Payback & How It Works

Sedra Electric · 10 August 2026 · 6 min read

With rising electricity tariffs and Egypt's abundant sunshine, solar power has become one of the smartest investments a homeowner or business can make. This guide explains how solar works, the system types, the savings you can expect, and how to get started.

How solar power works

Solar panels convert sunlight into electricity during the day. That power runs your home directly, and any surplus can be sent back to the grid or stored in batteries. The result: you buy far less electricity from the utility, and your bills drop.

On-grid, off-grid or hybrid?

  • On-grid: connected to the utility. Cheapest and most popular for homes — you use solar by day and the grid at night, and net metering credits your surplus.
  • Off-grid: fully independent with battery storage. Ideal for remote sites with no reliable grid.
  • Hybrid: solar + batteries + grid — solar by day, stored power in the evening, grid as backup.
For most homes and businesses in Egypt with a stable grid connection, an on-grid system delivers the best value and the fastest payback.

What is net metering?

Net metering lets you export the extra electricity your panels produce during the day to the grid, and draw it back at night — effectively using the grid as a "battery." Your bill is based on the net amount, which is what makes on-grid solar so cost-effective.

Real savings & payback

A well-designed system can cover a large share of your electricity consumption, cutting your monthly bill significantly. Because tariffs keep rising, the savings grow over time — and a quality system keeps producing for 20+ years. The exact payback depends on your consumption, roof space and system size.

The honest way to know your numbers is a site assessment: we review your bills and roof, then size a system and show you the expected savings and payback for your case.

What affects the cost

  • System size (kW) — driven by how much you consume.
  • On-grid vs battery storage (batteries add cost).
  • Panel and inverter quality.
  • Roof type and installation complexity.

How to start

  • Gather your recent electricity bills.
  • Request a site assessment and a sized proposal.
  • Choose quality components with a solid warranty and local support.
  • Confirm the net-metering paperwork is handled for you.
