Structured Cabling & Home Networks: Build a Network That Lasts

Sedra Electric · 10 August 2026 · 5 min read

Every smart home and modern building runs on one invisible foundation: the network. Cameras, automation, Wi-Fi, TVs and phones all depend on it. Get the cabling right and everything just works; get it wrong and you'll fight dropouts and dead zones for years. Here's what structured cabling is and why it matters.

What is structured cabling?

Structured cabling is a planned, standardised network of cables and hardware that connects every point in a building back to a central location. Instead of messy, one-off wiring, everything follows one organised system — making it fast, reliable and easy to expand or troubleshoot.

Why Wi-Fi alone isn't enough

Wi-Fi is convenient, but it's only as good as the wired network behind it. Thick walls, distance and interference create dead zones. A proper design uses wired backbones feeding well-placed access points, so you get strong, stable coverage everywhere — not just next to the router.

A great network is designed once, during construction — running cable after the walls are finished is expensive and disruptive. Plan it early.

What a good network includes

  • Structured cabling to every room and device point.
  • A central rack housing the switch, router and patch panel.
  • Well-placed access points for seamless Wi-Fi coverage.
  • Capacity headroom so the network handles future devices and higher speeds.

Planning tips

  • Design the network during the building phase, not after.
  • Add more cable points than you think you need — they're cheap now, costly later.
  • Use quality components and proper certification/testing.
  • Keep the central rack accessible and well-ventilated.
